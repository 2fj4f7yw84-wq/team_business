A business case study

Can Nike grow more responsibly?

External pressure has pushed Nike to change how it makes and sells products. This investigation asks how far those changes go for people, the planet and the business.

01 / THE COMPANY

A multinational with a long supply chain

Nike sells athletic shoes, clothing and equipment around the world. It designs and markets products across countries, while independent suppliers manufacture them in different locations.

That makes Nike a multinational business. Its choices affect factories, workers and communities outside the country where the company is based.

Its scale also makes sustainability complicated. Changing the energy and materials used by suppliers requires coordination across a large network.

$46.3bnNIKE, Inc. revenue in fiscal 2025, down 10% from fiscal 2024. Revenue changes alone do not show whether sustainability helped or hurt sales. [1]
02 / EXTERNAL PRESSURES

Why is Nike changing?

01

Customer expectations

Some buyers want products made with less waste and lower emissions. Their choices can affect brand reputation and demand.

02

Investor demands

Shareholders have asked for more detail about Nike's plans to meet climate targets. A proposal for additional disclosure was rejected in September 2026. [6]

03

Rules and scrutiny

Environmental reporting rules and independent investigations encourage large firms to explain their social and environmental impacts. Legal applicability varies by market. [7] [8]

03 / BUSINESS TOOL

Triple Bottom Line

The Triple Bottom Line tests a business in three connected areas. It helps us judge whether Nike's progress is balanced.

People

Factory workers, Nike employees and local communities are affected by employment, working conditions and production decisions. [5]

Planet

Energy use, materials, waste and manufacturing emissions make up the environmental side of Nike's decisions. [2]

Profit

New practices require investment. Nike must maintain a viable business while trying to reduce its impacts. [1]

04 / SUSTAINABILITY PRACTICES

What Nike reports doing

Energy and manufacturing

Nike is reducing emissions from its own facilities and working with suppliers on lower-emission production. It reported 96% market-matched renewable electricity coverage for its operations in FY24. [4]

Materials and waste

The company says it is developing lower-carbon materials, changing product design and reducing waste. The effect of an individual product does not show the effect of all products sold. [2] [3]

05 / STAKEHOLDERS

Who is affected?

Workers and suppliers

Changes to factory energy, materials and production affect supplier costs and workers' jobs and conditions. [5]

Customers

They decide whether Nike's products and environmental claims deserve their trust.

Communities

Communities near production sites can be affected by local environmental and social impacts.

Investors

Some want evidence that Nike has a realistic path to its climate targets. [6]

Employees

They have to develop and implement the changes. Reporting on cuts to sustainability teams raises questions about capacity. [8]

Governments

They set reporting and due diligence expectations for companies operating in their markets. [7]

06 / CHALLENGES AND JUDGEMENT

Real progress, open questions

Nike reports large emissions reductions, including across its supply chain. That is measurable progress, although these headline figures come from Nike's own reporting. [2]

Changing independent suppliers is difficult and can cost money. Nike's FY25 revenue fell, and independent reporting described cuts to its sustainability staff. Neither point alone proves whether the strategy will succeed or fail. [1] [8]

External pressures have influenced Nike considerably. Its environmental results show progress, but long-term success also depends on workers' outcomes, reliable reporting and financial performance.

This is an evaluation of the evidence, not a claim that any one stakeholder caused all of Nike's changes.
