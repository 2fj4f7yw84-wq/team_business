Business sustainability · case study

Nike under pressure.

To what extent have external pressures influenced Nike to adopt more sustainable business practices, and how effective have these changes been in balancing economic growth with environmental and social responsibility?

Company: NIKE, Inc.Business tool: Triple Bottom LineEvidence: company data + independent reporting
01 / Company overview

A global footprint

Nike designs and sells athletic footwear, clothing and equipment. It is a multinational because its products are sold across regions worldwide while manufacturing is carried out through independent suppliers in different countries. Decisions about materials and production therefore affect people and environments far beyond its U.S. headquarters.

Its scale gives Nike buying power and global reach, but also makes its supply chain difficult to monitor. In fiscal 2025, Nike reported $46.3 billion in revenue, down 10% from the prior year. Revenue alone cannot show whether sustainability caused growth or decline. [1]

02 / Business tool

Triple Bottom Line

This tool tests performance across people, planet and profit. Progress in one area does not automatically mean success in the others.

PEOPLE

Workers and communities

Nike's supplier factories create employment, while concerns about wages and working conditions make supplier oversight an ongoing responsibility. Investors have also raised worker-protection questions. [5]

PLANET

Emissions and materials

Nike reports major emissions reductions and is working on lower-carbon materials and manufacturing. Its global production network remains the hardest part to change. [2]

PROFIT

Growth and costs

New materials and supplier changes require investment. Nike links efficiency and innovation to business resilience, but FY25's lower revenue means sustainability cannot be credited with proven growth. [1] [3]

03 / Specific practices

What Nike is doing

Energy and emissions: Nike is shifting its own facilities toward renewable electricity and working with suppliers on lower-emission energy and manufacturing. In FY24 it reported 96% market-matched renewable electricity coverage in its operations. [4]

Materials and waste: Nike says it is integrating lower-carbon materials and design improvements into products and working to reduce waste. The environmental effect depends on total production and verified full-life-cycle impacts, not just individual products. [2] [3]

04 / External pressure & affected stakeholders

Who has a stake?

Customers

Buyers may value more responsible products and can challenge environmental claims. Their choices affect Nike's reputation and sales.

Factory workers & suppliers

Suppliers face pressure to change materials, energy and production; workers are affected by employment, pay and conditions. [5]

Investors

Some shareholders seek clearer climate plans. In September 2026, shareholders rejected a climate-disclosure proposal backed by Norway's wealth fund, showing that investor pressure is real but contested. [6]

Governments

Rules on reporting and supply-chain due diligence raise expectations for large companies. Applicability depends on the law and company scope; this is a regulatory pressure, not proof that a specific rule applies to all Nike operations. [7]

Communities & environmental groups

Communities can experience manufacturing impacts, while advocacy and investigation can call attention to gaps between targets and practice. [8]

Nike employees

Teams develop and implement sustainability measures. Reported cuts to Nike's sustainability staff raised questions about the resources behind its commitments. [8]

05 / Challenges and effectiveness

Progress, with limits

What the evidence supports

Nike reports substantial reductions in operational and supply-chain emissions. Its work with suppliers and materials suggests sustainability has become part of business decisions. These figures demonstrate reported progress, though they come from Nike's own reporting. [2]

Outside pressure is visible in investor demands, scrutiny from journalists and the wider policy environment. These pressures likely reinforce Nike's response; the available evidence does not establish exactly how much each one caused the changes. [6] [7]

What remains difficult

Changing suppliers' energy use and production requires coordination across a large network. Investment costs and falling sales add financial pressure. ProPublica reported cuts to sustainability staff, while shareholders rejected a request for further climate-plan disclosure. These facts make it harder to judge whether Nike's current pace and accountability will be enough. [1] [8] [6]

Judgement: External pressures have influenced Nike to a considerable extent, alongside its own business goals. Its reported environmental gains are meaningful, but the social results and long-term balance between profit, people and planet need continued independent scrutiny.
